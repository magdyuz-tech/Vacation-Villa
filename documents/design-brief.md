# Architectural Design Brief

**Five-Storey Vacation Villa** — Ezbit Abu Rukbah, Quesna, Menoufia, Egypt.

Written from the drawings rather than before them: every figure below is measured from the same geometry the set is drawn from, so the brief describes the building that exists rather than the one that was intended.

## The site

- Plot 264.5 m², an L on a street frontage 23.00 m wide.
- The house sits hard against the east boundary with a 1.00 m paved side passage, and the whole 11.50 m entrance garden on the west. It is not in the middle of its plot, and the drawings show that rather than a symmetrical fiction.
- Neighbours: west 3 storeys and blind toward the garden, east 1 storey in clay brick 1.00 m away, north 1 storey. The east wall can be glazed at all because of that single storey: the lowest east sill at +4.20 clears its roof by 1.20 m.
- Setbacks of 1.00 m on three sides, which are our own land and are paved as side passages rather than left dead.

- A private stair, 18R at 183 × 260, in an open well with no enclosure and no lift.

## The street front

Scheme A, "Wave", chosen from three. A serpentine cantilever of one full period across the 9.50 m frontage, 1.95 m deep at each end and 1.35 m at the waist — 15.7 m² a floor, 62.7 m² over the four levels that carry it.

The waist was 0.75 m when the scheme was adopted and was deepened to 1.35 m so the balcony is usable along its whole length rather than only at the ends. That cost the curve half its swing and gained 2.9 m² a floor.

The balconies stop at the corner on every level: the 1.00 m setbacks forbid anything projecting west, north or east, so the cantilever stays on the street frontage.

## What the drawings do not decide

- A measured topographic and boundary survey (SUR-001) is not in hand; boundaries on A-000 are issued marked indicative.
- The ground is central Nile Delta, which is well characterised as a region — enough to assume a foundation type and lay the structure out, not enough to size it. Allowable bearing pressure, the water table and sulphate content are plot figures, and GEO-001 stays open until they arrive with a source. What the layout assumes in the meantime is written down on GEO-002, each figure with its basis: regional typicals with no source, and a water table the client gives as confirmed. They are assumptions, they are stamped as assumptions wherever they print, and nothing is sized from them.
- The north neighbour is a single storey, confirmed by the client. The north wall carries windows on all five levels; only the ground floor looks at anything, and from +3.30 up they clear its roof. Its exact height and position still come with SUR-001.
- The exterior is settled: scheme A, “Wave”, adopted at P18. Schemes B and C still build and stay in the repository as the record of what was offered, stamped not selected and left out of the issue.
- Sheets A-300, A-301 and A-302 are withdrawn at P22, being enlargements of apartments the villa does not have. They still build and stay in the repository as the record, stamped on the face and left out of the issue.
- ARC-600 draws four junctions figured from the geometry, and names against QS-003 every build-up it does not specify. Until those topics are answered no detail on it can be built, priced or warranted, and no quantity is taken from it.
- The governing fire code is named at P30 — Code 305, the Egyptian code for protecting structures from fire, enforced through Civil Protection — and naming it is all that is done. No requirement from it is written on any sheet: its text could not be read here, the edition in force is unconfirmed after the Ministry of Housing's March 2026 decision on a new fire-prevention code, and whether a detached single-family dwelling is treated separately from a building of several households is the question that decides whether one unenclosed stair to five storeys is a problem at all. That question goes to Civil Protection or to an engineer licensed to answer it. A code is not chosen, and there is no laxer one to adopt.
- There is no way up to the roof over the penthouse. The stair runs from the ground floor to the penthouse and stops there — it is drawn “dn” only on A-104 — and the deck at +16.45 carries the water tanks, which somebody has to reach to clean, fill and replace. A hatch and ladder, or a last short flight, is a design decision with a hole in a slab behind it, so it is recorded here rather than drawn on an assumption. Found at P29 while measuring the stair, which had never been measured.
- Rates are class X throughout. No rate appears in any document here without a named source, and QS-004 is issued with its rate column open.

The set is issued for the owner's record, costing and construction information. It is not a tender issue and it is not for construction.
